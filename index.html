  • Act 3, the fix. 7:30 to 8:45.
  • We've solved this once already. Agentic coding quietly moved the authoring step back to everyone-brings-their-own.
  • What goes in the factory, in one breath: everything between a person and the change. That's the agent and model, your prompts and rules files, skills, MCP servers (the tools an agent can call), and the checks that run before anything merges.
  • We standardized the path to production, not your editor. Same here: share the factory, keep your hands.

  • Act 4, the call. 12:45 to 14:00. If the clock is past 13:30 leaving slide 13, skip the per-item notes and just read the five words.
  • You're all doing it differently, but there is so much middle ground if you align on what's best for the product and what's best for the enterprise. And almost nobody has claimed it yet.
  • Skills: the shared ways your team gets things done.
  • Flow: how a story moves from the board to merged.
  • Validation: how you know a change does what it says.
  • Evaluation: how you know the agent's output is good before a human looks.
  • Improvement loops: how every fix and lesson flows back into the factory.
    • Validation and self-improvement

    Stop asking what the perfect tool is. Ask your product to examine itself.

    "Pull up the last 100 PRs and pipelines. Identify common errors, failures, successes, bottlenecks, etc. and identify a list of core updates that would make the next 100 better."

    Congrats, you just built a self‑improving factory.

  • 14:00 to 15:00. Prompt on the first press, punchline on the second.
  • Validation and self-improvement sound like the hard ones. They're really just a matter of perspective.
  • Everyone's out shopping for the perfect eval tool. Skip that for now. Point the agent at your own history and ask it what would make the next batch better.
  • Read the prompt out loud. It's one prompt, and anyone in this room could run it tomorrow.
  • Take the top few updates it suggests, check them into the repo, and run it again next month. That's the improvement loop.
