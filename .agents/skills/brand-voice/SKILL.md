# Brand voice

## Voice

- Plain, spoken English. If it sounds odd said out loud to a room, rewrite it.
- Dry, self-aware humor. The speaker is in on the joke and usually the target of it ("If that stings a little, good. It stung me too.").
- Optimistic. Every problem slide is followed by a way through.
- Hot takes are bold on purpose. Sharpen them; don't soften them. Only reword one if the room would misread what it means.
- Notes are written in first person, as cues the speaker can glance at: a time window first, then one idea per line.

## On screen

- One idea per slide. If a slide needs a paragraph, the paragraph belongs in the notes.
- Big statements get a slide of their own.
- Build steps reveal one line per press for anything the speaker wants to land separately.

## Look

- Light industrial: warm gray background, near-black ink, one amber accent, hazard stripes on the first and last statement slides.
- Display font for statements, body font for lists, mono for labels, code and the slide bar.
- Accent is used sparingly: list markers, highlights under a punchline, the one thing to look at.
